Manali

Beas river light, cedar groves and the valley that changes with every season

Kullu Valley Base

One valley, several different holidays

Manali sits where the Beas widens under the Pir Panjal. The Mall handles the everyday bustle; three kilometres up, Old Manali keeps the slower café afternoons. Higher still, Solang opens into a bowl used for snow sports in winter and meadow activities once the snow lifts. Rohtang, when the pass is open, is a separate high-altitude day — not a default add-on for every itinerary.

Most travellers do well with three nights here: a Solang day, a town-and-temple day, and one flexible morning for Old Manali or a tunnel-side outing. How the week feels depends far more on the month than on stacking extra stops.

Altitude ~2,050 m3 nights typicalAdventure & café base

Famous for

  • Solang Valley for snow weeks, ropeway views and seasonal adventure sports
  • Hadimba Devi Temple in its cedar grove above the town
  • Old Manali cafés along the Manalsu stream
  • Atal Tunnel day drives toward Sissu when conditions allow
  • Vashisht hot springs and easy Beas riverside walks

Best time to visit

  • March to April: blossom in the orchards, quieter trails, Rohtang usually still closed
  • May to June: the full summer valley — passes tend to open, hotels are busiest
  • October to November: the sharpest mountain light and thinner crowds after the rains
  • December to February: snow in Solang and occasional falls in town — plan for cold and traffic spikes around holidays
How the year feels

Manali through the seasons

Spring

Apple and cherry blossom, rhododendron on the higher slopes, a valley still waking up.

Summer

Green hillsides, open high roads and the classic first-visit window — book stays early.

Autumn

Clear Pir Panjal views, apple harvest and the month many regulars prefer.

Winter

Deodars under snow, wood-stove cafés and a Solang day built around the weather.
