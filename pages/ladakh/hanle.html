Hanle

A thin village under one of India’s darkest usable skies

Changthang plateau

Go for the night, not only the drive

Hanle sits on a high, dry plateau in eastern Ladakh, far past the usual Nubra–Pangong loop. The Indian Astronomical Observatory is why the sky here is protected. The village and the small gompa are why the day before dark is not empty. This is not a first-week add-on. It wants its own two or three days after you have already slept high.

The road is long. Nights are cold even in summer. Clear, moon-poor dates in late summer and early autumn are the reason most people come. Combine with Tso Moriri only if the week is long enough for both high nights.

Dark-sky plateau2 nights typicalRemote add-on

Famous for

  • One of the darkest practical night skies in the country
  • The high-altitude observatory on the plateau
  • Hanle gompa above a small Changthang village
  • A quieter east than the Pangong shore
  • Wildlife and empty roads for travellers who already know Leh

Best time to visit

  • August to October: the clearest, driest nights for the Milky Way
  • April to May and September: good sky, fewer summer vehicles
  • June to July: reachable, but the sky is not always the point
  • November to March: extreme cold; not a casual add-on
