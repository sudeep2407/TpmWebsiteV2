Leh

The high town that every Ladakh circuit should start in, not rush through

Indus valley base

Arrive, acclimatize, then look around

Leh sits close to 3,500 metres on a shelf above the Indus. The palace still holds the ridge, the old lanes drop toward the market, and the first two days here are not wasted sightseeing — they are how the rest of the week stays possible. Fly in if you can. Drive in only if you had enough time to acclimatize on the Manali or Srinagar road.

A local day covers the palace, Shanti Stupa at dusk, and one nearby gompa such as Thiksey or Hemis. Do not put Khardung La or Pangong on day one. The altitude does not negotiate.

Altitude ~3,500 m2–3 nights firstYear-round by air

Famous for

  • Leh Palace on the ridge above the old town
  • Shanti Stupa and the evening light over the Indus
  • Nearby gompas — Thiksey, Hemis, Shey — within an easy half-day
  • The market lanes that still work as a real town, not only a stop
  • The only honest acclimatisation base before Nubra, Pangong or Hanle

Best time to visit

  • April and May: kinder crowds than peak July–August
  • June to September: all side valleys open; Leh is the working base
  • October to March: Leh is open by air; some high altitude roads still uncertain; most circuits shut after October
How the year feels

Leh through the seasons

Late spring

Passes start to open. Leh is quiet, cold at night, useful if you fly in early.

Peak summer

Long days, full rooms, every side trip on the map.

Early autumn

The clearest air of the year and a thinner crowd.

Winter

A closed-road town. Flights still land. Circuits do not.
