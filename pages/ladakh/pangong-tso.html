Pangong Tso

A long salt lake that changes colour with the sky, not a two-hour stop

~4,350 m lake

Famous water, still a high-altitude day

Pangong Tso sits east of Leh across Chang La, a brackish lake that runs far beyond the Indian shore. The colour — mineral water under thin air — is why it is on every first itinerary. The drive is five to six hours. The lake is not a picnic after a flight the same morning.

One night on the shore is the honest plan if you want dawn on the water. A same-day return from Leh is possible after you have already slept high. Inner Line Permit applies. The Indian stretch is the only part of the lake this circuit uses.

Altitude ~4,350 mVia Chang LaILP required

Famous for

  • A high salt lake whose colour shifts with cloud and sun
  • The Chang La crossing on the way out of Leh
  • Dawn and dusk on the Indian shore when you stay overnight
  • A first-timer landmark that still needs acclimatisation
  • A busier bank than Tso Moriri — plan the hour you arrive

Best time to visit

  • Late May to September: the lake is open and the pass is in use
  • June and September: strong colour, thinner crowds than July–August
  • July to August: fullest camps, busiest shore
  • Winter: the lake ices; the road is not a standard tourist circuit
How the year feels

Pangong Tso through the seasons

Late May

Water thawed, camps opening, a quieter bank.

Peak summer

The colour most photographs promise — and the most vehicles.

September

Clearer peaks and a better overnight if rooms remain.

Winter

A frozen sheet. Only for travellers who already know the cold.
