Offbeat Sikkim

Quieter corners of Sikkim — Yuksom, Aritar Lake, Chayataal and Nathang Valley

West & East Sikkim

Four quieter places beyond the main towns

Away from Gangtok’s high street, Sikkim has several smaller stops that reward an extra night. Yuksom, in West Sikkim, was the state’s first capital. It is known for Dubdi Monastery, the Coronation Throne and as the traditional gateway for treks toward Kangchenjunga. Aritar, in East Sikkim, is a village beside a forest lake, often visited with the Silk Route.

Chayataal is a high, still water body on the eastern ridges. Nathang Valley is an open highland above Zuluk, used more as a sunrise viewpoint than as a town. The east-side places usually need permits. October to May is the practical season; Yuksom remains reachable in the monsoon when the high valleys do not.

YuksomAritarChayataal & Nathang

Famous for

  • Yuksom — first capital, Dubdi Monastery and trek trailhead
  • Aritar Lake and its surrounding village
  • Chayataal, a high lake on the eastern ridges
  • Nathang Valley’s open pastures and sunrise views
  • A slower look at Sikkim outside the main circuit

Best time to visit

  • October to May: the best window for both west and east
  • Yuksom: reachable most of the year, including wetter months
  • Aritar: pleasant from autumn through spring
  • Nathang and Chayataal: better in the dry months; winter snow can close the road
How the year feels

Offbeat Sikkim through the seasons

Autumn

Clear views on the Silk Route and in West Sikkim.

Winter

Yuksom stays open; Nathang depends on snow.

Spring

Mild weather on both the east and west sides.

Monsoon

Forest around Yuksom; high valleys are often in cloud.
