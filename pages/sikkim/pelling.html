Pelling

A small West Sikkim town known for close views of Kangchenjunga

West Sikkim

West Sikkim’s mountain balcony

Pelling is a small hill town in West Sikkim, set at about 2,150 metres, with a direct outlook toward Kangchenjunga — the world’s third-highest peak. On a clear morning the snow wall fills the horizon from hotel terraces and roadside viewpoints. The town itself is quiet compared with Gangtok: a short bazaar, lodges and a handful of cafés.

Close by are Pemayangtse Monastery, one of Sikkim’s oldest, the ruined palace of Rabdentse, and the sacred Khecheopalri Lake in the forest. Many travellers also visit Yuksom, the first capital of Sikkim, from here. The mountain is most often visible from October to December and again from March to May.

Altitude ~2,150 mKangchenjunga viewsMonasteries

Famous for

  • Sunrise views of the Kangchenjunga range
  • Pemayangtse Monastery and Rabdentse ruins
  • Khecheopalri Lake, sacred to local communities
  • Skywalk and ridge viewpoints above town
  • A quieter base than the state capital

Best time to visit

  • October to December: the clearest mountain views of the year
  • March to May: good visibility and spring colour on the slopes
  • January to February: cold mornings, often still clear
  • June to September: cloud and rain hide the range on most days
How the year feels

Pelling through the seasons

Autumn

Sharp light on Kangchenjunga after the rains.

Spring

Milder days and flowering forests around town.

Winter

Cold air and frequent clear mornings.

Monsoon

Green hills, but the mountain is usually hidden.
